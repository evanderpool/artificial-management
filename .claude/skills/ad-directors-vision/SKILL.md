# Ad Director's Vision Skill

Produce a full **Director's Vision** document for conversion-focused video ads. This is not just a script — it's a complete creative and strategic brief that aligns brand, emotion, cinematography, structure, pacing, and affiliate/conversion goals into one cohesive plan.

---

## When to Use This Skill

Any time the user wants to:
- Plan or brief a video ad (TikTok, Instagram Reel, YouTube Short, Facebook Ad, UGC, etc.)
- Write a director's brief or creative treatment
- Develop a product video concept
- Create an affiliate or performance marketing video
- Think through visual storytelling for a brand or product

---

## Information to Gather First

Before writing the vision, collect (ask the user if not provided):

1. **Product/Brand**: What is the product? What's the brand name/vibe?
2. **Platform & Length**: TikTok 15s? Instagram 30s? YouTube 60s?
3. **Target Audience**: Who are we speaking to? (age, lifestyle, pain point)
4. **Tone**: Energetic? Calming? Aspirational? Humorous?
5. **Any existing assets?**: Footage, logo, tagline, previous ads?

If the user gives you the product name and basic context, proceed with reasonable assumptions and note them clearly.

---

## Output Format: The Director's Vision Document

Produce the following 7 sections in order. Be specific, visual, and actionable — write like a real creative director briefing a production team.

---

### 1. 🎯 Product & Brand Focus

- State the product's **core benefit** in one sentence
- Describe how it aligns with the **brand identity** (aesthetic, values, tone)
- Identify the **problem it solves** and what makes it **different from competitors**
- Define the product's hero moment: what does it look like at its best?

---

### 2. 💡 Core Message & Emotional Arc

- Pin down the **single emotional takeaway**: Should viewers feel empowered, relieved, delighted, confident, or seen?
- Define the **story arc** in 3 beats: Setup → Shift → Resolution
- Write the **core message** as one line (the thought viewers should leave with)
- Identify the **tone**: Is this aspirational, relatable, urgent, playful?

---

### 3. 🎬 Visual Style & Cinematography

Define the look and feel of every frame:

- **Color palette**: (e.g., warm earth tones, high-contrast neon, soft pastels, clinical whites)
- **Shot types**:
  - Close-ups for intimacy/product detail
  - Wide/lifestyle shots for context
  - POV shots for immersion
  - Fast cuts for energy vs. lingering shots for emotion
- **Lighting style**: Bright & airy / Moody & dramatic / Soft & natural / Studio-clean
- **Motion style**: Handheld for authenticity / Gimbal for polish / Slow-mo for impact / Speed ramp for hype
- **Aesthetic reference**: Name a visual mood or comparable (e.g., "Glossier campaign feel" or "gritty iPhone vlog aesthetic")

---

### 4. 🏗️ Structure: Scene-by-Scene Breakdown

#### Hook (0–3 seconds)
The most critical moment. Choose one:
- **Bold product reveal** (product appears dramatically)
- **Problem scenario** (viewer sees themselves in the problem)
- **Surprising or pattern-interrupt moment** (unexpected visual or statement)
- **Direct address** (UGC style: "I can't believe this worked…")

Describe exactly what the viewer sees and hears in these 3 seconds.

#### Main Product Highlight (Middle section)
- **Lifestyle demo** (product in real-world use — aspirational or relatable setting)
- **Feature close-up** (dramatic angles showing the product's best physical traits)
- **Before/after** (transformation moment)
- **Social proof insert** (text overlay, review snippet, result stat)

Describe the key shot(s) and what makes them compelling.

#### CTA — Call to Action (Final 3–5 seconds)
- What is the **spoken or on-screen CTA**? (e.g., "Link in bio", "Shop now — 20% off today only")
- Does it feel **natural or urgent** — which does this video need?
- Is there a **visual anchor** for the CTA? (product shot, URL, discount badge)

---

### 5. ⏱️ Pacing & Length

- **Total length**: (15s / 30s / 60s — and why this length fits the platform and message)
- **Rhythm**: Fast-cut energy (cut every 1–2s) OR narrative pace (3–5s per beat)
- **Number of scenes/beats**: List them (e.g., Hook → Problem → Solution → Demo → CTA = 5 beats in 30s)
- **Transition style**: Hard cuts / Fade / Whip pan / Match cut

---

### 6. 📊 Ad Manager Perspective

Think like a media buyer:

- **Primary persona**: Who is this ad targeting? Describe them (age, lifestyle, pain point, platform behavior)
- **Ad placement context**: Is this a cold-audience top-of-funnel video or a retargeting warm-audience ad?
- **Variations needed**: Should we shoot multiple hooks for A/B testing? (Recommended: 2–3 hook variants)
- **Brand consistency check**: Does the tone match previous campaigns? Any brand guidelines to honor?
- **Performance signals to watch**: What metric signals this creative is working? (Hook rate, watch time, CTR, conversion)

---

### 7. 💰 Affiliate & Conversion Focus

Close the loop on revenue:

- **Problem-agitation**: What specific pain does the video name or show?
- **Solution clarity**: Is it immediately obvious how the product solves the problem?
- **Key benefits** (list 2–3 that should be visible or spoken)
- **Urgency mechanism**: Limited time offer / Exclusive deal / Low stock / Seasonal — make it feel real, not forced
- **Trust signal**: Review, stat, guarantee, or celebrity/influencer endorsement to include?
- **Affiliate-specific note**: If this is affiliate content, ensure the offer is crystal clear and the CTA drives to a trackable link or landing page

---

## Closing Summary

End the document with a **Director's One-Liner**: A single sentence that captures the entire vision.

> *Example: "A fast-cut, warm-toned 30-second Reel that opens on a relatable morning struggle, pivots to effortless confidence with the product, and closes with a discount-urgency CTA — targeted at women 25–40 who want results without complexity."*

---

## Formatting Notes

- Use headers for each section
- Use bullet points within sections for scannability
- Be specific — avoid vague descriptors like "nice" or "good vibes"
- Write shot descriptions as if briefing a cinematographer
- If the user wants a script instead of (or in addition to) the vision doc, offer to write one after the vision is complete
